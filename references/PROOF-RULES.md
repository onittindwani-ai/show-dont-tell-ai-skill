# Proof Rules

Show, don't tell only works if people believe what they see. Proof is what makes them believe. Fake proof does the opposite: it gets found out, it destroys trust, and in many countries it breaks consumer protection and advertising law, plus the rules of Google, Meta, Amazon and most review sites.

## The proof ladder (strongest first)

| Level | Type | Example | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Named customer result with permission | "Priya's bakery went from 3 to 11 online orders a day in 6 weeks." | Strongest. Always get written permission (a WhatsApp "yes" counts). |
| 2 | Real numbers you can back up | "1,240 homes cleaned since 2019." "4.8 stars from 312 Google reviews." | Use the exact number. Keep the source (invoices, the review page). |
| 3 | Real review, word for word | "'They fixed it in 40 minutes and left the place cleaner.' Ravi K., Google" | Quote exactly. First name plus initial, plus where it came from. |
| 4 | A real guarantee | "Not fixed in 48 hours? The visit is free." | Only if you'll honour it every time. State the terms. |
| 5 | Visible process | "You get a written quote within 24 hours." "Photos before and after every job." | Real if you really do it every time. Good when you have nothing else yet. |
| 6 | Credentials | Licence number, certification, award, membership | Only if current and really held. |
| 7 | Specific detail | "Beans roasted every Monday in Bengaluru." | Detail itself builds belief, because vague businesses can't give it. |

Use the highest level you truly have. Two pieces of proof near the top of a page beat ten at the bottom.

## Never do this

- Invent numbers, percentages, reviews, names, logos, ratings, awards, years or "as seen in".
- Round up ("almost 500" when it's 312, "10+ years" when it's 8).
- Write a review yourself, ask friends to post fake reviews, or pay for reviews.
- Use a client's name, logo or photo without permission.
- Turn one customer's result into a promise: write "One client saw..." not "You will see...".
- Use "up to" to hide that the typical result is much smaller.
- Promise outcomes in health, legal, finance or education.
- Show "results" from before the business existed or from a different business.

## Marking missing proof

Put the marker exactly where the proof should go, and say what's needed:

```
[PROOF NEEDED: 1 short review from a customer about how fast the work was done]
[PROOF NEEDED: the exact number of jobs done since you started, counted from invoices]
[PROOF NEEDED: permission from the clinic to use their name and result]
```

The person can publish without a marker filled only if they delete that line. Never publish a marker.

## The 2 week proof plan

| Day | Do this | Gets you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Count real numbers from invoices, bookings, orders or your phone: customers served, jobs done, years. Write the exact numbers down. | Level 2 proof |
| 1 | Write down 3 things you do every time (response time, quote time, follow up, photos, clean up). | Level 5 proof |
| 2 | Send the review request below to your 10 happiest recent customers. | Level 3 proof |
| 3 to 7 | On your next 2 jobs, take a before and after photo or note the before and after number. | Material for level 1 |
| 7 | Ask those customers for permission to share their result with their first name. | Level 1 proof |
| 8 | Decide one guarantee you can afford to honour every time. Write it in one sentence with clear terms. | Level 4 proof |
| 14 | Replace every `[PROOF NEEDED]` marker. Test the new version (see TESTING.md). | |

## Messages you can copy

**Asking for a review (WhatsApp or email):**
```
Hi [name], thank you again for choosing us for [job]. Could you reply with 1 or 2 lines about what changed for you after [job]? I'd love to quote you on our website, with just your first name. No pressure at all.
```

**Asking permission to share a result:**
```
Hi [name], your [result, for example "orders going from 3 to 11 a day"] is a great example of what's possible. Could I share it on our website as "[the exact sentence]"? I'd use your first name and business name only. Happy to change anything.
```

**Asking for a Google review (with the direct link):**
```
Hi [name], if you were happy with [job], a Google review would help us a lot. It takes 30 seconds: [link]. Thank you!
```

## Quick legal and platform notes (not legal advice)

- Many countries treat fake or misleading reviews and claims as illegal (for example the FTC rules in the US, the CMA in the UK, and consumer protection law in the EU and India). Platforms remove fake reviews and can penalise the business.
- If you give customers anything in return for a review, many rules require you to say so, and some platforms ban it entirely. Asking for an honest review is fine.
- Before and after photos in health and beauty may have extra rules. Check yours.
- When in doubt, cut the claim or ask a lawyer.
