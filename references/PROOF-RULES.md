# Proof Rules

Proof is what makes "show" believable. Fake proof destroys trust the moment someone checks, and in many countries it is illegal (consumer protection and advertising laws, and the rules of Google, Meta and most review sites).

## What counts as proof (strongest first)

1. **A specific result for a real customer**, with their permission: "Priya's bakery went from 3 to 11 online orders a day in 6 weeks."
2. **A count you can back up:** clients served, projects done, years, orders, reviews.
3. **A real review**, quoted word for word, with first name or initials and where it came from (Google, email, WhatsApp).
4. **A guarantee you really offer**, in exact terms: "If it's not fixed in 48 hours, the visit is free."
5. **A visible process detail:** "You get a written quote within 24 hours", "We send photos before and after every job."
6. **A recognised badge, licence or award** that the business really holds.

## Never

- Invent numbers, percentages, reviews, client names, logos, ratings, awards or guarantees.
- Round up ("almost 500" when it's 312).
- Turn one customer's result into a promise for everyone. Write "One client saw..." not "You will see...".
- Use a client's name or logo without their permission.
- Promise outcomes in health, legal or finance.

## When there is no proof yet

Leave a marker: `[PROOF NEEDED: a short review from a recent customer]`. Then give the person a plan:

| Proof to collect | How (in 2 weeks or less) |
| --- | --- |
| 3 short reviews | Message 5 happy customers: "Could you reply with 1 or 2 lines about what changed for you? I'd love to quote you on my site." |
| 1 before and after | Next job: take a photo or note the number before and after. Ask permission to share. |
| A count | Count from your invoices, bookings or orders. Use the exact number. |
| A guarantee | Decide what you can really promise and still make money. Write it in one sentence. |
| A process detail | Write down what you already do every time (response time, quote time, follow up). That's proof. |
