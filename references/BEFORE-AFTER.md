# Writing a Before and After People Can Feel

A before and after lets the reader see themselves in the story. It's the fastest way to show value without bragging.

## The camera test

Could you film it? If yes, it works. If no, it's too abstract.

| Fails the camera test | Passes the camera test |
| --- | --- |
| Before: inefficient processes | Before: you type every order into 3 spreadsheets at 11 pm |
| After: improved productivity | After: the orders are in before you finish your coffee |
| Before: poor online presence | Before: someone searches "plumber near me" and finds your competitor |
| After: peace of mind | After: you stop checking your phone at dinner |
| Before: cash flow issues | Before: 4 clients owe you money and you're too awkward to ask |
| After: better cash flow | After: invoices go out and get paid without you chasing |

## The 5 ingredients (use 2 or 3)

1. **A time:** "at 11 pm", "every Sunday", "in the week before tax day".
2. **A place:** "at the counter", "in the car park", "on your phone at dinner".
3. **A number:** "3 spreadsheets", "4 clients", "2 hours".
4. **An action:** "type", "chase", "search", "redo", "apologise".
5. **A feeling, shown not named:** not "frustrated" but "you sigh and open the laptop again".

## 4 shapes that work

### 1. The 2 line contrast (homepages, profiles)
```
Before: [specific bad moment today]
After: [the same moment with you]
```

### 2. The day in the life (emails, proposals)
```
Today, [what their Monday looks like].
With [you], [what Monday looks like].
```

### 3. The number shift (B2B, results driven)
```
[metric] today: [real number]. [metric] after: [real or target number].
```
Only use a real result, or label it clearly as a goal ("Our goal for you: 15 a month").

### 4. The customer story (strongest, needs permission)
```
[Name] used to [before]. Now [after]. "[Their words]."
```

## Rules

- **Be fair to the before.** Describe the customer's situation with respect. Never mock them.
- **Make the after realistic.** Typical results, not your best ever case. If it varies, say "most customers" only if true, or "one customer".
- **Same moment, both sides.** The before and after should describe the same situation (the same Monday, the same counter), so the change is obvious.
- **Short.** 1 or 2 lines each. If it needs a paragraph, it's not sharp enough yet.

## Examples by industry

| Business | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gym | You pay every month and go twice | You've got a 7 am slot with your name on it, and a coach who texts if you miss it |
| Tutor | Homework time ends in tears at the dining table | Your child does the sheet alone and asks for the next one |
| Wedding photographer | You get 2,000 photos in a Google Drive and never look at them | You get 1 album you show everyone at Diwali |
| IT support | The printer stops and the whole office waits | You message us, and it's fixed before your tea is cold |
| Restaurant | Friday dinner means 40 minutes in a queue | You book a table on WhatsApp and walk straight in |
| Real estate agent | 15 viewings, 15 homes that looked better in photos | 3 viewings, all homes that match your list |
| Software tool | Your team copies data between 4 tools every day | The data moves itself; your team works on customers |
