# Template: Sales Email (Cold, Warm and Follow Up)

A sales email is read on a phone in about 8 seconds. It gets a reply when it's about the reader's problem, believable, and asks for something tiny.

## The structure

```
SUBJECT     The result or their problem, 3 to 8 words, lower case feels personal
LINE 1      Their situation today, in their words (shows you understand)
LINE 2      What changes with you (the result)
LINE 3      One piece of real proof
LINE 4      One tiny ask, answerable in seconds
SIGN OFF    Your name, role, 1 line, no long signature
```

Target: under 90 words. No attachments in a first email. No images.

## Fill in version: cold email

```
Subject: [their problem or the result, 3 to 8 words]

Hi [first name],

[Their situation today: "Most cafes I talk to lose Saturday mornings to the online order backlog."]
[The result: "We set up ordering so orders print in the kitchen by themselves, no tablet juggling."]
[Proof: "We did this for 40 cafes in Pune this year, including [name, with permission]."]
[Tiny ask: "Worth a 10 minute look on Thursday?" / "Reply YES and I'll send a 1 page plan for your cafe."]

[Your name]
[Role, business, 1 line]
```

## Fill in version: warm email (they enquired or met you)

```
Subject: [the thing they asked about] for [their business]

Hi [first name],

[Reference the moment: "Great talking at the expo about your delivery delays."]
[The result for them specifically: "Here's how we'd get your orders out by 6 pm every day."]
[Proof: one similar customer result, or process detail]
[One ask: "Shall I hold Tuesday at 11 for a 20 minute walk through?"]

[Your name]
```

## Fill in version: follow up (no reply)

Never "just following up". Every follow up brings something new.

```
Subject: [new angle or new idea]

Hi [first name],

[One new useful thing: an idea, a result, a short tip for their business.]
[One yes or no question: "Still worth looking at, or should I close your file?"]

[Your name]
```

Follow up rhythm that respects people: day 3, day 7, day 14, then stop. Each one shorter.

## Subject lines that work (and why)

| Subject | Why |
| --- | --- |
| "your books, done every month" | The result, plain and personal |
| "Saturday orders at [cafe name]" | Their name plus their problem |
| "idea for [their website]" | Curiosity, and it's a gift |
| "3 enquiries a month?" | Their number, as a question (only if you know it) |
| "quick question, [first name]" | Low pressure (use sparingly) |

Avoid: "Partnership opportunity", "Introducing [your company]", ALL CAPS, emojis, "Re:" when it isn't a reply.

## Line by line guidance

- **Line 1 is about them.** Not "My name is...", not "I hope you're well". Prove you know their world in 1 sentence.
- **Line 2 is one result.** Not 5 services. The single change they'd care about most.
- **Line 3 is proof, not praise.** A number, a similar customer, or a specific process. If you have none, use a specific detail of how it works.
- **Line 4 is tiny.** A yes, a time, a 1 word reply. Asking for a 1 hour meeting from a stranger fails; asking "worth a look?" works.
- **Personalise one line for real.** Mention one true, specific thing about their business. Mass emails with fake personalisation get spotted.

## Common mistakes

- 3 paragraphs about your company history.
- A PDF brochure attached to a cold email (often goes to spam, rarely opened).
- "Let me know if you're interested in learning more" (vague, easy to ignore).
- Several links and several asks.
- Fake "Re:" or fake urgency.
